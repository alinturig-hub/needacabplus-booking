# Three-source traffic correlation and driver-62 fallback

The Operations traffic map correlates Street Manager, TomTom and Plymouth Council reports by normalized road names, nearby reported geometry (75 m tolerance) and overlapping published periods. Each group retains source-specific references, details and warnings. Distinct reports from the same provider, ambiguous multiple matches, archived records, missing geometry and stale records remain separate. Conflicting ended/current reports are marked for review, not promoted to a confirmed live closure. Source cards count raw records; the list counts related groups. A match is evidence of related reports, not a guaranteed shared work identifier.

The live notification scope remains **driver ID 1364 / callsign 62 only**. Add runtime `COUNCIL_DRIVER62_ALERTS_ENABLED=true` to opt into Council comparison alongside the existing pilot and TomTom settings. `live` mode or another configured driver does not enable Council. The planner applies the same fresh GPS, current vehicle/shift, approach, ban-list, cooldown and final send checks as the existing pilot.

Council-only automatic alerts require all of:

- A successful Council publication check within 12 hours, with a current stored notice checked within 12 hours. A failed refresh pauses Council alerts even while a previous snapshot is displayed.
- A fresh successful TomTom comparison. A failed/missing TomTom feed does not establish that a Council event is absent there.
- No related Street Manager or TomTom report in the same place and period, including a related completed/cancelled Street Manager record. This avoids turning an old scheduled notice into a new live alert.
- A verified reported junction or between-junction segment. Named-street centroids, unmapped notices and ambiguous geometry cannot send. Verification means a unique OpenStreetMap topology match to the reported location, not confirmation of works on site.
- Valid published dates and explicit current hours. Supported schedules include stated 24-hour periods, explicit AM/PM or 24-hour clock ranges, overnight ranges and stated weekday-only restrictions. Ambiguous/multiple hours, holidays, cancellations and unspecified hours remain excluded/review. Calendar and overnight windows use Europe/London, including BST.

Council messages identify the source and use **scheduled**, for example `Council notice: a scheduled road closure nearby on North Road West. Check signs.` They do not independently assert that a road is currently physically closed. Current Street Manager/TomTom candidates have priority over Council-only scheduled notices. The worker adds the existing `[TRAFFIC TEST]` prefix.

Messages use a shared normalized road/locality key and retain provider/legacy aliases for daily suppression. If an already reported event acquires a different source identity, a `SUPPRESSED / SOURCE_DUPLICATE` audit marker remembers the shared key without an Autocab call or cooldown extension. Claim-time checks recheck equivalent keys transactionally. Different alert kinds retain escalation: a works warning cannot consume a later closure warning; a prior closure suppresses lower-severity warnings for its related road keys. Cross-provider matching still depends on the names and geometry available; it cannot reliably identify unrelated or nonoverlapping reports as the same event.

Council synchronization now checks every 15 minutes. Successful publications remain on a six-hour fetch interval; errors respect the existing 15-minute backoff and preserve the last successful inventory. Verified previous positions are retained, approximate positions may be upgraded when a unique reported junction or unambiguous between-junction path is resolved. The parser rejects unexpectedly truncated inventories.

If the Council website responds 403 from the server, source status remains stale and Council-only automatic alerts remain paused. The map explicitly shows that condition and the last successful check. Do not promote an old snapshot to current or treat provider unavailability as corroboration. Street Manager and TomTom continue independently. No automated message is sent simply by viewing the map, and archive snapshots never qualify for notifications.

A trusted operator who can access the official HTTPS publication can refresh it through the existing authenticated maintenance endpoint `POST /api/internal/street-manager/supplementary-sync`, with body `{councilSnapshot:{html,fetchedAt}}`. This uses the existing job-secret header, not public or passenger access. Fetch the official page immediately before submitting: snapshots older than five minutes or from the future are rejected before database access. The same parser, inventory-drop guard and geometry checks apply. Store the fetch time, SHA-256 and `operator_snapshot` provenance; retain the prior direct-fetch error. The map marks operator-checked snapshots and unavailable automatic access explicitly. This supplies a current checked publication, not a claim that the server's direct refresh has recovered. A later failed scheduled refresh pauses Council alerts again; a successful direct refresh replaces the operator provenance.

No new database migration is required; operational alias markers use the existing delivery audit table. Runtime flags persist across restarts, delivery deduplication remains in SQL, and TomTom's transient single-driver feed still requires a fresh response after restart. This remains the authorized single-driver pilot rather than a wider fleet rollout.
